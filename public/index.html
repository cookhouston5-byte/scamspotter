What it targets

The scams aimed at small businesses

Tuned for the messages that reach the people who pay bills, run payroll, and answer the front desk.

Phishing & fake logins

“Your Microsoft 365 password expires today.” A link to a fake sign-in page.

Lookalike links · spoofed brands · “verify your account”

Fake invoices

Bills for work you never ordered, or a real vendor’s invoice sent by someone else.

Overdue pressure · risky attachments · mismatched senders

Payment-change fraud

“We’ve changed banks.” One reply can redirect a large payment for good.

New bank details · wires · crypto · deposit changes

Gift card scams

A text “from the owner” asking staff to buy gift cards quickly and quietly.

Gift card asks · secrecy · free email accounts

Fake support & renewals

“Your subscription renewed for $499. Call to cancel.” The number reaches a scammer.

Callback numbers · remote-access tools · surprise charges

Scam texts & chats

“Unpaid toll: pay today to avoid a fine.” “Your card is locked. Reply YES.” “Is this still your number?”

Toll & delivery lures · lock alerts · job offers · wrong-number openers

FAQ

Questions

How accurate is it?

ScamSpotter looks for the warning signs that show up again and again in real business scams, in English and Spanish messages. It catches a lot, but no tool is perfect. Treat “Safe” as one input, not a guarantee. For anything involving money or passwords, verify through a channel you trust.

Do you store the messages we check?

No. The checker analyzes the message to produce a result and doesn’t save the message content. If optional AI analysis is turned on, the text is sent to that AI provider for the check. Don’t paste passwords or full card numbers.

How does my team get unlimited checks?

When you start the trial, you get a team access key on screen and by email. Share it with your staff. Each person pastes it once under Menu → Your plan, and that browser gets unlimited checks. No individual accounts needed.

How do I check on or cancel my subscription?

Open the Menu (top right) and go to Your plan. It shows whether you’re on the trial, when it ends, and your next renewal date. Tap “Manage subscription” to update your card, download invoices, or cancel.

Can I check a screenshot of a text or chat?

Yes. Tap “Screenshot” and pick the image. ScamSpotter reads the words right on your phone or computer, so the image is never uploaded, then checks them. It works for text messages, WhatsApp, Facebook and LinkedIn messages, and pop-up warnings. Look over the text it read, since a letter can occasionally be off. The result lists any links it found so you can compare them with the original.

Does it work in Spanish?

Yes. ScamSpotter checks messages and screenshots in English and Spanish (for example, “peaje pendiente”, “tarjeta bloqueada”, or “tarjetas de regalo”). The explanation of the result is in English for now.

Can I put ScamSpotter on my phone?

Yes, no app store needed. On iPhone, open scamspotter.app in Safari, tap Share, then “Add to Home Screen”. On Android, open it in Chrome and tap “Install app” (or Menu → Add to Home screen). On Android you can then share a text or screenshot straight to ScamSpotter from the Share menu.

What does the Agent do?

The Agent is a deeper check for Team plan members. Paste an original email and it checks who really sent it (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), where replies and links really go, attachments, and how old each domain is. Upload a photo or video and it looks for signs of AI generation or face swaps. Or describe a phone call and it checks for voice-clone scam scripts. It explains everything in plain English. No tool can prove a photo or video is real, so treat it as one strong signal.

Can staff forward emails instead of pasting them?

A forwarding inbox (forward a message, get the verdict by reply) is in the works and will be included in the Team plan. Today, staff paste the message into the checker.
